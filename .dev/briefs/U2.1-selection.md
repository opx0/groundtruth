# U2.1 — the selection and ordering policy

`docs/BRIEF.md` B7 is the spec. This is the judgement tier of the report: it
decides which records appear, in what order, and which template a record gets.
The templates deliberately cannot branch, so every such decision has to live
here, where it is one named function with a test on it.

Nothing else in the codebase is allowed to make these decisions. If you find
yourself wanting a conditional inside a template or inside the report route,
that conditional belongs in this file.

## Reading order

1. `docs/BRIEF.md` B7 (the spec), B2's boundary table (what each source's
   boundary is and how it is displayed), A2 screen 3 (the sentences a card
   shows and their order), B10 (failure wording), C2 (words that never appear).
2. `lib/evidence/sentence.ts` — `SectionSpec`, `SectionFilter`, `defineSection`,
   `sectionOrdering`, and every `*Placement` type. Read `render` and `trace` so
   you know exactly what a placement has to carry.
3. `lib/evidence/templates.ts` — `SectionSubject`, `Reported`, and the comment
   about why a count is never a number anyone wrote down.
4. `lib/templates/` — every file. `sems.ts`'s module comment states, in its own
   words, that choosing between its four templates is this unit's job and what
   the input to that choice is.
5. `lib/adapters/sems.ts`, `lib/adapters/echo.ts`, `lib/adapters/fema.ts`,
   `lib/adapters/frs.ts` — the fields you are ordering and filtering on, and
   what is really null in the recorded bytes.
6. `lib/report/grouping.ts` — the shape of a `GroupingResult`, which you turn
   into group placements.

## What you build

One new file, `lib/report/selection.ts`, and one new test file,
`tests/unit/report/selection.test.ts`.

A pure function of `(store, outcome, …)` to placements. No fetching, no
clock, no randomness, no mutation, and no numbers that reach the screen.

### The plan holds no counts

This is the rule that matters most, and it is not negotiable.

A count on screen is the length of a section's ordering, recomputed from the
store at render time. That is what makes "15 sites" drop to "14 sites" when a
record is removed, with nothing else touched. A count copied into a plan object
is a number that can drift away from the records behind it, which is the exact
failure `lib/evidence/sentence.ts`'s module comment was written to prevent.

So: your plan carries `SectionSpec`s and `Placement`s. It never carries a
count, a total, a "showing N of M", or any other number destined for a
sentence. The route computes what it needs from the store at render time, via
`sectionOrdering`, right next to where it renders the count.

### Shape

Design the exact types yourself, but they must express these things and the
compiler must enforce them:

- A **card** per source. It always carries a `SourcePlacement`, because B7
  requires the status of every source to appear whether or not it has records.
- **Headline placements**: the section-scoped count and no-data sentences, in
  reading order. B7 says counts come before records. Each carries its own
  `SectionSpec`, because each count has its own filter.
- **Listings**: a `SectionSpec`, the order the records are in, the records
  shown, and the records carried but collapsed.
- A listing entry is **one record and the sentences it renders**, not one
  sentence. A FEMA record needs several sentences; an ECHO facility needs
  several. B7's "up to five records" counts records, so the shape has to make
  that countable. A record with no sentences at all must be impossible to
  construct.

### The four Superfund templates

`lib/templates/sems.ts` has four. Choose between them from
`record.statusRow`, which the adapter set to exactly one of three states, plus
one comparison:

- `unavailable` → the status-unavailable wording, and only that one. The other
  three each assert something about what the Superfund inventory answered, and
  after a failed request nothing about that is known.
- `no-row` → the registry-only wording.
- `joined` → the summary, unless the two agencies disagree, in which case the
  disagreement wording.

"Disagree" needs your judgement and a documented rule. The recorded bytes say
this: Envirofacts `npl_status_name` and the FRS layer's `ACTIVE_STATUS` carry
the same three values in different capitalisation — `Not on the NPL` versus
`NOT ON THE NPL`, `Currently on the Final NPL` versus
`CURRENTLY ON THE FINAL NPL`, `Site is Part of NPL Site` versus
`SITE IS PART OF NPL SITE` — and all fifteen recorded Houston sites agree.
Check that yourself against `tests/fixtures/sems/` before you write the rule.
Whatever you decide, the comparison may only decide which template renders. It
may never change a rendered value: both fields still print exactly as their
agency sent them.

### The final-NPL sentence

B7: SEMS records are ordered by distance, and NPL sites are *also* named in
their own sentence. Both, not either — nothing removes a record.

Filter the final-NPL section on the **Superfund** field (`semsNplStatus`), not
on the registry field. Both agree in all fifteen recorded sites, and the
Superfund inventory is the system whose answer that sentence is about.

Note what that filter cannot see, and say so in your module comment: a site
whose status request failed has a null Superfund status and therefore cannot be
counted as final-NPL. That is the honest outcome — it is not known to be on the
list — but a reader deserves to have it stated, and the section trace lists the
ids counted, so the two numbers can be reconciled.

`Currently on the Final NPL` is the value for the final list.
`Site is Part of NPL Site` is a different value and is not it; the Houston
point has three sites carrying an NPL-ish status and `docs/BRIEF.md` A2 says
two are on the final list. Your test must pin that distinction.

### The ECHO order

B7: records with a formal enforcement action; then records in current
noncompliance; then by distance; ties by newest first. Plus, from B7's last
line: unknown values sort after known values and stay visible.

Two of those keys need a field decision, and both have already been made in
this codebase — match them rather than inventing a third rule:

- **Has a formal enforcement action**: `lastFormalActionDate` is non-null.
  `SectionFilter` grew a `{ field, present: true }` variant for exactly this
  count; use it. Do not use `formalActionCount`, which is ECHO's Clean Air Act
  column only and would undercount.
- **In noncompliance**: `quartersInNoncompliance` is at least 1. Read the
  comment above `echoNoncomplianceCount` in `lib/templates/sections.ts` for why
  this codebase counts on that column rather than on `complianceStatus`, and
  why the sentence says "twelve-quarter history" rather than "current". Your
  ordering must use the same rule the count uses, or the list and the count
  will describe different sets.

"Newest first" is the record's `effectiveAt`, which the adapter set to the last
formal action date falling back to the last inspection date. Null sorts last.

Make the sort total: after every stated key, break remaining ties on
`sourceRecordId`, so the same store always produces the same order. A test must
prove that.

### Bounds, and no silent truncation

B7 shows up to five records per section and offers "View all" for the rest.
ECHO's five-mile query at the demo point answers 1,686 facilities
(`docs/BRIEF.md` B14), so "the rest" cannot mean all of them: rendering 1,686
sentences with their traces would be a payload nobody can use.

So a listing carries a bounded number of records beyond the five it shows, and
the bound is a named exported constant with a comment justifying it. The count
sentence still states the true total from the store, because the count is the
length of the ordering and the store holds every record.

A bound that is not visible is a lie. Whatever a card ends up carrying, the
report must be able to state how many records it is showing out of how many
exist — the route will compute both from the store at render time. Your job is
to make that possible: expose the listing's spec so the total is derivable, and
make the carried count derivable from the listing itself. Do not bake either
number into the plan.

### What always appears

B7's list, checked one by one. Your test asserts each:

matched-address precision; the status of every source; the FEMA result or the
FEMA no-polygon state with its dataset; the AirNow result or its no-data state;
the nearest qualified AQS monitor per pollutant; ECHO and SEMS counts before
records; up to five ECHO and five SEMS records.

AQS and AirNow have no adapter yet and no record kind in play. Build the card
functions for them anyway, against their record kinds, so that wiring an
adapter later is a registration and not a redesign. Say in your report what you
could not test because no fixture exists.

### FEMA

The flood card takes `FloodZoneResult` from `lib/adapters/fema.ts`
(`floodZoneOutcome`), not a slot of a shared fan-out. `.dev/PLAN.md` queue item
8 says why: the no-data note has to be able to say which of the two datasets
answered, and the fan-out's note cannot. `FloodZoneResult` carries the dataset
beside the outcome, and each adapter already declares its own `noDataNote`, so
your section's `note` comes from the outcome, never from a string you write.

`FloodZoneResult.nfhl` is non-null when the authoritative layer failed and Esri
answered instead. That failure is a fact about the report and the reader is
entitled to it. Decide how it surfaces and say why.

Nothing else.

Read everything. Write nothing else. Do not edit `lib/evidence/**`,

## Acceptance

Every item is a command or an assertion, not an opinion.

1. `pnpm verify` green. Paste the real output.
2. Tests build records from the committed fixtures through the real adapters
   and the kernel's `complete`, never from invented payloads.
3. A test proves each of the four Superfund templates is chosen for a record in
   the state that calls for it, including the `unavailable` state, and that the
   status-unavailable record never renders the registry-only wording.
4. A test proves the final-NPL section counts exactly the two Houston sites
   `docs/BRIEF.md` A2 names, and that the `Site is Part of NPL Site` record is
   not one of them and is still present in the main listing.
5. A test proves the ECHO order against the seven recorded facilities: a record
   with a formal enforcement action outranks a nearer record without one, and a
   record with quarters in noncompliance outranks a nearer record with none.
6. A test proves unknown values sort last and are still in the output.
7. A test proves the order is total: shuffle the input, get the same order.
8. A test proves no record is ever dropped from a listing's `shown` plus
   `rest` except by the stated bound, and that the bound is what dropped it.
9. A test proves removing one record from the store lowers the rendered count
   by exactly one, through a placement the policy produced.
10. A test proves every source gets a status placement, including one that
    answered with no data and one that was unavailable.
11. Zero type assertions (`as`), non-null assertions (`!`) or `any`.
12. `bash .dev/census/mutation-check.sh` still reports 7 of 7.

## Forbidden

No new dependencies. No network. No git commands — leave the tree dirty.
No `Date.now()` or `new Date()` in the policy: retrieval times come from the
outcomes.

## Report, 25 lines maximum

The two files. Real `pnpm verify` and mutation-check output. The rule you chose
for "the two agencies disagree" and the evidence for it. Your bound on carried
records and why that number. One line per acceptance item saying which test
proves it. Everything you wanted to say and could not, with the field that was
missing. Anything you think is wrong with this brief.

# Addendum, written after the templates landed

The brief above was written before `lib/templates/{echo,fema,frs}.ts` existed.
Everything in it still holds. This is what changed under it.

## The kernel now checks your choice

`defineTemplate` takes a fourth argument, a list of `Requirement`s, and
`assemble` refuses to render a template whose subject does not satisfy them.
Read `Requirement` in `lib/evidence/templates.ts` and `satisfies` in
`lib/evidence/sentence.ts` before you write a line.

This changes the shape of your mistakes. Choosing the wrong template used to
produce a false sentence; it now produces **silence**. That is better, and it
is still a defect: a record the report holds and cannot describe is a record
the reader does not see. So your tests must assert, per record, that the
placements you produce actually render — not that they are non-empty.

## The templates, and what each one demands

**`sems-site`** — four, exported from `lib/templates/sems.ts`. Each declares
its state, so your job is to pick the one that matches and the kernel will
catch you if you do not:

| Template | Requirement |
|---|---|
| `semsSiteSummary` | `statusRow` is `joined` |
| `semsSiteRegistryOnly` | `statusRow` is `no-row` |
| `semsSiteStatusUnavailable` | `statusRow` is `unavailable` |
| `semsSiteNpl` | `semsNplStatus` equals `Currently on the Final NPL` |

`sems-site/disagreement@1` has been **deleted**. The brief above told you to
write a rule for when the two agencies disagree; do not. `ACTIVE_STATUS` on the
`FRS_INTERESTS_SEMS` layer is the Superfund interest's own status and equals
`npl_status_name` upper-cased on all fifteen recorded sites, so no recorded
record is in that state. `lib/templates/sems.ts`'s header records why, and
`.dev/PLAN.md` queue item 16 is what would bring it back.

`semsSiteNpl` is a second placement for a record that also appears in the main
listing. Nothing removes a record.

**`echo-facility`** — six, exported from `lib/templates/echo.ts`, and they
divide into primaries and secondaries. This division is the thing to get right:

- **Primaries**, exactly one per record, carrying the facility's name and its
  distance: `echoFacilitySummary` (requires `complianceStatus` present) and
  `echoFacilityNoStatus` (requires it absent). They are disjoint and
  exhaustive, so every record has exactly one.
- **Secondaries**, any number, each naming the facility inside its own clauses
  so it reads correctly wherever it is placed, and each rendering null when it
  has nothing to say: `echoFacilityFormalAction` (requires
  `lastFormalActionDate` present), `echoFacilityNoFormalAction` (requires it
  absent), `echoFacilityNoncompliance` (requires `quartersInNoncompliance` at
  least 1), `echoFacilityIndustryCodes` (no requirement; renders null when both
  code columns are null).

A card must place the primary first. Placing two primaries would print the
facility's name twice, which is the defect `7f2ac98` was written to remove —
do not reintroduce it from this side.

**`fema-flood-zone`** — two, from `lib/templates/fema.ts`: `floodZoneSummary`
(requires `sfhaLabel` present) and `floodZoneUnmappedFlag` (requires it
absent). Disjoint and exhaustive on that slot, so place both and let the
kernel drop the one that does not fit, or pick on `sfhaLabel` yourself; say
which you chose and why.

**`frs-facility`** — two, from `lib/templates/frs.ts`, neither with a
requirement. `frsFacilityIdentity` is the facility as its own subject;
`frsFacilityCrossReference` is the identity printed under a record from another
source that this registry ID resolved. Which one a placement gets is your
decision and depends on where the card puts it.

**No `aqs-monitor-summary` or `airnow-observation` templates exist yet.** Their
adapters are briefed in `.dev/briefs/U1.6-U1.7-air.md` and blocked on a key the
operator has to register. Build their card functions anyway, as the brief above
says, and say in your report exactly what you could not test.

## The ECHO counts, pinned

`section/echo-formal-actions@1` and `section/echo-noncompliance@1` are already
written and their wording is settled. Your filters must match the rules the
templates were worded for, or the count and the list will describe different
sets:

- formal actions: `{ field: "lastFormalActionDate", present: true }`
- noncompliance: `{ field: "quartersInNoncompliance", atLeast: 1 }` — the same
  threshold `echoFacilityNoncompliance` requires, and the reason
  `section/echo-noncompliance@1` says "at least one quarter of noncompliance in
  ECHO's twelve-quarter history" rather than A2's "current noncompliance".

And the ECHO **order** must use those same two rules, in B7's order, so the
records at the top of the list are the records the counts counted.

## The FEMA no-data note

`FloodZoneResult` carries the dataset beside the outcome, and each flood
adapter declares its own `noDataNote`. A `no-data` outcome already carries the
right one. Your section's `note` comes from that outcome and never from a
string you write.
