`tests/e2e/` is empty. `playwright.config.ts` is written, Chromium is

## How the app is driven, and why not live

The eight paths above are all user-visible states, and reaching them from live
sources would make the suite a weather report. From this machine ECHO is slow
and flaky, FEMA's NFHL host refuses the connection outright, and AQS and AirNow
have no key — four of the eight paths would be untestable and the other four
would be timing-dependent.

So intercept **our own two API routes at the browser**, with `page.route()` on
`**/api/geocode` and `**/api/report`, and serve bodies the **real handlers**
produced over the **committed fixtures**.

That is the whole design, and the part that matters is where the body comes
from. Playwright's test process is Node, so a test can import
`createReportHandler` and `createGeocodeHandler` and the fixture-backed
`SourceIo` they are already tested against, run them, and fulfil the route with
what came back. The bytes the browser sees are then produced by the route code
this repo ships, over bytes a government endpoint really sent — not a
hand-written body that will drift the first time a sentence changes.

`tests/unit/app/helpers/report-stream-fixtures.ts` already does the report
half: read it first. It exports plans (`DEMO`, `FOUR_STATES`, `withSlowEcho`)
and `reportResponse(plan)`, which is a real `Response` with a real NDJSON body.

Two rules this design has to keep:

- **Nothing under `tests/` may be imported by anything under `app/` or
  `lib/`.** `docs/BRIEF.md` B11 says recorded fixtures live only under the test
  directory and production code cannot import them, and a test asserts it. The
  interception is in the test process; the server that Playwright starts is the
  real production build and knows nothing about any of this.
- **No hand-written NDJSON, and no hand-written sentence.** If a path needs a
  state the existing plans do not produce, add a plan, do not write a body.

## The eight paths

One test per path, named for the path. For each, say in your report which
fixture produced it.

1. **A precise match.** 9311 E Ave P. The confirm screen shows the matched
   address and the block-not-parcel sentence, and it is the rendered origin
   sentence — assert the span carries its `data-field`, not just the text.
2. **An ambiguous match.** `tests/fixtures/census/ambiguous-100-main-st.json`
   is seven candidates across states. A choice is required: assert that no
   candidate is auto-selected and that choosing one reaches the confirm screen.
3. **A no-match.** `tests/fixtures/census/no-match-9400-clinton.json`. B10's
   wording, and the address still editable.
4. **A SEMS result with trace to raw field.** Click a Superfund sentence's
   span and assert the panel names the agency, the EPA site ID, the raw field
   and the raw value. This is A3's promise and the one path a judge will drive.
5. **An ECHO enforcement result.** The facility with a formal enforcement
   action is `110064116987` in the committed page fixture; assert the sentence
   and its trace.
6. **A FEMA polygon and a FEMA no-polygon state, each naming its dataset.**
   Two assertions in one test or two tests, your call. The two no-polygon
   wordings differ on purpose and both name their layer.
7. **A distant monitor.** The AQS card, with the monitor's distance beside its
   value. Another unit is registering AQS and AirNow as sources the report
   actually asks; if that has not landed when you start, say so and write the
   test against whatever the card currently is, marked `test.fixme` with the
   reason rather than deleted.
8. **One failed source with a usable report.** `FOUR_STATES` carries all four
   card states in one stream. Assert the failed card says so, and that the rest
   of the report is fully readable beside it — that is A6 beat 6.

## What to assert, and what not to

Assert what a reader sees and what a judge will click: the sentence text, the
`data-field` on a span, the panel's rows, the card's state. Do not assert class
names or DOM structure — those are the screen's business and will change.

Do not re-assert what the unit tests already prove. There are 617 of them and
they cover the rendering exhaustively. What Playwright adds is that the pieces
are wired together in a real browser against a real production build: the
stream is consumed, the panel opens, the flow moves between screens. A path
that passes in a unit test and fails here is a wiring bug, and wiring is all
this unit is looking for.

## Acceptance

1. `pnpm e2e` green. Paste the real output, including the count.
2. `pnpm verify` still green — you have added no unit test and broken none.
3. Eight tests, named for the eight paths, each failing if its path breaks.
   Say for at least three of them what you ran to confirm they fail.
4. No test depends on another's state, and the suite passes with
   `fullyParallel` as configured.
5. No hand-written response body anywhere in `tests/e2e/`.
6. Zero type assertions, non-null assertions or `any`.

## Forbidden

No new dependencies. No live network in any test — if a path cannot be reached
without one, that is a finding, not a reason to reach for the network. No git.
Do not edit anything outside `tests/e2e/` and `playwright.config.ts`; if a
selector you need does not exist, report it rather than adding it yourself.

## Report, 20 lines maximum

The eight paths, one line each: the fixture behind it and the assertion that
carries it. Real `pnpm e2e` output. Which three tests you proved can fail, and
how. Anything you could not reach and why.
